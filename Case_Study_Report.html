Case Study: Replacement of Conventional PI Controller with Intelligent Control Architectures for DC Bus Voltage Regulation

An Engineering Investigation into Closed-Loop DC Microgrid Stability under Sudden Disturbances
Document Type: Technical Case Study Report
Target Setpoint: 300.0 V DC Bus
Dataset: Case Study DCbusData (120,001 Samples)

1. Executive Summary

Voltage regulation across the DC link capacitor is fundamental to the stability of DC microgrids, solar/battery storage units, and EV charging infrastructure. The primary control objective is to maintain the sensed DC bus voltage (V) strictly at the 300.0 V reference setpoint, mitigating transient sags, surges, and ripple caused by sudden load switching.

Conventionally, a fixed-gain Proportional-Integral (PI) controller generates the converter control command. However, analysis of plant operational data reveals significant limitations: the PI controller exhibits sluggish response and notable error excursions under severe disturbances. This case study evaluates the complete replacement of the linear PI controller with two non-linear intelligent control paradigms: a 2-Input Mamdani Fuzzy Logic Controller (FLC) and an Adaptive Neural Network (ANN) Controller. Dynamic simulations demonstrate that intelligent control achieves an >72% reduction in tracking error (RMSE) and an 80% reduction in cumulative absolute error (IAE).

2. Problem Statement & Plant Context

Consider a power converter supplying a common DC bus with capacitance C. The governing dynamic equation of the DC link voltage is given by the current balance:

C · (dVdc / dt) = iconverter(t) − iload(t)

Where iconverter is modulated via the controller output u(t), and iload(t) acts as an external, unmeasured load current disturbance.

Critical Engineering Constraint: This investigation is not an open-loop time-series forecasting problem. Predicting future values without feedback does not stabilize hardware. The objective is active feedback regulation: computing u(t) in real time to force the tracking error Verr(t) = V* − Vdc(t) → 0 as rapidly as possible.

5. Intelligent Control Architectures

Mamdani Fuzzy Logic Controller (FLC): Employs 2 inputs [Error e(t), Rate of Change de/dt] and 5 linguistic sets: NB (Negative Big), NS (Negative Small), ZE (Zero), PS (Positive Small), and PB (Positive Big). Centroid defuzzification provides smooth corrective damping.

PB ZE PS PB PB PB

Adaptive Neural Network (ANN) Controller: A feedforward network (3 inputs [e, de/dt, ∫e dt], 6 hidden tanh neurons, 2 output neurons) dynamically adapts proportional and integral gains in real time to minimize tracking loss J = ∫ (V* − Vdc)2 dt.
